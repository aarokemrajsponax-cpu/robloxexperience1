# Maison Noir — getting the word out

How to bring people to the house and keep them coming back, with everything ready to paste or
upload. Nothing here can promise numbers: what Roblox shows to whom depends on how players
behave once they're in, and that's the part the game itself has to win. What this plan does is
put the game in front of the right people honestly, and measure what works.

The game page itself (name, description, genre, questionnaire, icon, thumbnails, video, friend
referral banner) is in `LAUNCH.md`. This file is everything after that.

---

## 1. What decides who sees the game

Roblox's Home page ("Recommended For You") ranks games on how players who found the game
**there** behave afterwards. Roblox's own list of the signals that matter most:

| Signal (Creator Analytics → Discovery) | What the house does about it |
| --- | --- |
| **Play-through rate**: how often seeing the tile turns into a play | The icon and the first thumbnail. Test them (thumbnail testing in Creator Hub) and keep the winner. |
| **First-play bounce** (leaving within 60 s, or 1–3 minutes) | A new guest lands in the lit Foyer, not a menu; the objective says "Take a seat at any table in the Salon"; the Concierge offers the tour. Watch this number after every update. |
| **Play days per user** (day 1, days 2–7, days 8–28) | The Nightly (one table for everyone, every day), the Evening and Week Commissions, the Ledger's pages, seasons, the Ballroom's evenings. |
| **Playtime per user** (counted up to 60 minutes a day) | Rooms to move up through, duels, the Suite, the Terrace. |
| **Intentional co-play days**: coming back to play *with friends* | Invite key, Library duels with a friend, trading, the Ballroom, Soirées. This is the signal invites feed. |
| **Qualified play sessions, spend days** | Honest offers only, never pay-to-win: revived points never count for the boards. |

Two more things from Roblox's discovery guidance worth keeping in mind:
- Pages whose pictures don't match the game, and pages that lead with giveaways ("free
  Robux"), are shown to fewer people. Every picture in `marketing/` is drawn from the game's own build:
  the same rooms, furniture and screens a guest sees (in Roblox the lighting is richer).
- Players who come from ads, invites, search or social links don't feed the Home ranking
  directly: they're for bringing in the first players. What makes Roblox recommend the game is
  how those players (and the Home-page ones after them) stay.

**Where to look:** Creator Hub → your experience → **Analytics** → Discovery (Home
Recommendations) and Retention. Write down D1 and D7 retention and first-play bounce before
and after every update.

## 3. Launch week, day by day

**Day 0 (publish day)**
1. Everything in `LAUNCH.md`: page words, icon, thumbnails, questionnaire (with **paid item
   trading: yes**), products, switches.
2. Play it yourself on a phone and on a computer for ten minutes. Check the first minute feels
   good: you spawn in the light, the objective is clear, a table is easy to find.
3. Invite three or four friends with the **Invite** key and play together: duel in the Library,
   trade a curio, watch a Ballroom evening.

**Day 1**
4. The referral banner: Creator Hub → Engagement → **Referral Rewards** (words in `LAUNCH.md` §6).
   Roblox lets you publish it once the game has been live for a day.
5. An **Experience Event** for the first Ballroom evening (section 4 below).

**Days 2–4**
6. Post the first short video (section 5) and the announcement (section 6).
7. Read Analytics: first-play bounce, D1 retention. If bounce is high, watch a friend's first
   two minutes and fix what confused them before spending anything on ads.

**Days 5–7**
8. If D1 retention looks healthy, run the first small ad test (section 7).
9. Ship a small update with what you learned, and post its notes (section 8).

## 4. Experience Events (Creator Hub → Engagement → Events)

Events show on the game page and remind players who choose "Notify me". Make them real
things that happen in the house:

| Event | When | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lights Out: the opening night** | your first busy evening, 1 hour | Twenty tables, one Ballroom. The lamps go out one by one until one is left. Bring a friend to the Gallery. |
| **Golden Hour** | weekly, 30 minutes (start it from House controls) | Every Gilt counts double for everyone in the house. |
| **The Soirée** | weekends | Your best evenings this weekend count. |
| **A new season** | when the season turns | The house dresses for the season: new pieces, new curios. |

Never schedule an event that doesn't actually run in the game at that time.

**Announcement (Roblox group, Discord, X/Bluesky):**

> cases before the clock runs out, duel a friend in the Library, and survive Lights Out in the
> Ballroom. Furnish your own Suite, fill your Vault, and meet everyone on the Terrace.

**Update post:**
> The house got grander again: every room is 1.6 times its old size, the Suite has a marble

## 7. Ads on Roblox (Creator Hub → Ads Manager)

Roblox's own ad tool can show the game as a sponsored tile on Home and in Search, or as ads
inside other games. An ad account needs a Roblox account aged 13 or older; paying by card needs
18 or older (younger creators can buy ad credits with Robux). Start small and measure:

1. **Test**: one sponsored-experience campaign, a small **daily** budget for 3–5 days, with the
   cover thumbnail. Let Roblox set the bid.
2. **Judge it on**: cost per play, and above all **D1 retention of the players the ad brought**
   (Analytics, by traffic source). Cheap plays that leave in a minute are worth nothing.
3. **Keep or stop**: if ad players come back the next day about as often as everyone else,
   keep the budget and try a second thumbnail; if they don't, stop and improve the first
   minutes of the game before spending more.
4. Never run ads with pictures or words that aren't in the game.

Spending on ads can't buy a place in Recommended For You; it brings players in, and their
behaviour does the rest.

## 8. Update notes

Post a short note with every update (the game page's description can carry the latest line).
Ready for this one:

> **The Grander house.** Every room is now 1.6 times its drawn size, and you walk a little
> quicker to match. **Your Suite** has a marble chimneypiece with a real fire, a gilded
> overmantel, tall lacquered front doors with your name in gold over them, and furniture a size
> up, dressed to the last detail. **The fireworks are a pyromusical**: three acts, each to its own
> score, from Japanese chrysanthemums to Niagara Falls pouring down the front of the house and
> the house's monogram written in gold in the sky. **The Golden Transformation** is now the
> Midas touch: your whole Suite turns to gold, coins rain, a choir sings and a crown settles on
> your head (only you see your Suite and its shows; Salutes are for the whole house). Salutes
> shine brighter too: real gold leaf, a followspot with rose petals, a wave of chandeliers, and
> a midnight that chimes. **The grand piano** sounds again, the first note to the last.
